When you learn multiple languages and deeply, you find that certain expressions are slightly different language by language. I'm interested in the language of `Love`. 

The expression for `Love` is quite interesting because it also closely related to the real feeling of love experienced by people. As I stay in the sphere of certain language, I begin to feel more what each word for `Love` feel. And by far of all the expressions that I know, Thương is the most special. 

By the way, this is the list of word for `Love` that I'm using for comparison: **yêu** (Vi), **Love** (Eng), **恋** (Koi - Jp) and **愛** (Ai - Jp).

1. Yêu and Love 

Yêu and Love are similar expression of Love. They are quite declaritive - which mean they are used in speech a lot, but also used to describe mundane feeling of love. They are both very clear-cut, very pure and somewhat time independent - which mean the speaker don't account for the momment when they use these word. They says things about the speaker but don't say things about the subject the speaker refering to. 

Ex: I love cat > You get that I have strong feeling for cat, and that's all I'm saying to you. 

2. **恋** and **愛**

With a 5 in Japanese, I can't say I will do these words justice, so please take my opinion with a grain of salt. **恋** is often used in the phrase **恋に落ちる** which literally mean to **fall in love** - so when you say it's more relate to a specific momment. While **愛** for me is more similar to Love. 

3. Thương 

Thương, for me is more interesting because it's says more than just your effection to that feeling - presumabbly just love. If you ask a Vietnamese person and if that person has to think seriously, they will tell you that Thương is somewhat relate to pityness. I thought about this notion before, but didn't quite believe it as true. Pitiness is an expression you give to people in their misfortune, but Thương is not like that. But I can't quite say how Thương is more 

When you say you Thương something, you are saying that you have affection for the person/thing, but also that you see how they are vulnerable. This can be mistaken to mean pity (cuz there isn't a word that mirror vulnerability in Vietnamese but when you know the word it makes a lot of sense). 

For example, when I say I Thương my mom, I mean that I care a lot for here, and I know she can be vulnerable when she have to burden a lot of work, how she have to live far a way from her hometown. 

> You are aware of the person/thing's vulnerability when you Thương them.

4. Closing

When I was small, I used to think saying Love/Yêu is cool. This is because in Vietnamese it's a lot easier to say Thương than to say Yêu. You don't have to be committed to anything thing when you say Thương, while saying Yêu is a lot a decraration and somewhat a commitment. But when I grow older, I realize that Thương is very special too, as a recognition of vulnerability, and a statement of empathy.
