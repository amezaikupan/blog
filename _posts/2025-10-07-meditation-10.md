I recently went on a 10-day Vipassana meditation course. With no irony, it's one of if not the most magical experience I've ever had. Here are some of my thoughts (without spoiling it too much)!

> Vipassana, which means to see things as they really are, is one of India's most ancient techniques of meditation. It was taught in India more than 2500 years ago as a universal remedy for universal ills, i.e., an Art of Living. (https://www.dhamma.org/)


# 1. I can't know until I have experienced it 

Before going on this trip, if you come up and tell me: "Meditation will change your life!" I would roll my eyes in disbelief. I went on the trip out of curiousity, you see. One of my teacher mentioned it in passing in a conversation, and I thought it's maybe a fun experience to try. I would never thought it's the answer to many of my problems. I **can't** imagine. 

During the course, I am taught that according to the Buddha, there are 3 types of wisdom. The first is Wisdom of hearing from others (include learning from textbook). The second is Wisdom from reasoning. The third is Wisdom of experience. They are 3 different level of understanding. Only through experience do you truly understand things - which is one thing I learned from the course. 

As an example, I've heard of the idea of `impermanence` for a long time, and I thought I understand it. But not until I have experience it (I won't tell you how) do I intuitively understand it. 

# 2. Observe ~ Calm ~ Good decision

You can only observe when you are calm, when you are balance. You can only make good decision when you are calm, when you just observe things. Otherwise, you are either going to favor something or against something out of momentarily emotion. You are going to be bias. 

Don't underestimate the power of just observe. It help you see clearly. You might think it's not important but that's because you think you see things clearly at all time, but in reality we barely see anything clearly most time. 

# 3. I learn something new everytime I meditate 

Either the meditation session goes smoothly or rocky. Usually, it's hard. But I learn that because I was able to observe things, I learn new things every session. Be it discovering a new area of pain on the body, a new sensation, a new reaction to a sensation, a new breathing (or just plain realization of how I usually breathe). Afterward, I learn something new.
 
# 4. You don't have to believe all the theory to enjoy the benefit 

I didn't believe in all the theory taught but I enjoyed the benefit of meditation a whole lot. And the theory isn't bad either. Learning the Buddha theories make me believe he was a very smart person. 

# 5. It's okay to start again, start again, start again

Normally, if I failed to do something, I would feel quite defeated and want to quit. I would think its something about me. But during meditation, that's something you just can't do. You can't quit during meditation. And so, each time you fail to observe and loss your balance, you start again, again, again. Sometimes more than 10 times in 1 minutes. 

I learn that starting over is easy if you don't carry the weight of the past. (Easier said than done)

# 6. My mind CAN be quiet 

I have live with a noisy mind for the majority of my life, and I can't imgine it being quiet. But now, I can. It can be quiet, and I can train it to. 

This is probably the most life altering thing that I have experienced so far. 

# 7. Goodness goes a long way 

The Vipassana course was carried out by a group of old volunteers. All the services provided to us are the work of old volunteers, and they only accept money donation from old volunteers. It amazed me how this seemingly fragile thing could last so long and still going strong. The support we were given during the course was huge. Before we come, there someone who have cleaned our bed. While we meditate, there are people who wake up early to cook for us, provide us with basic utility (from hair drier to ear pick). It's the best service I've ever received, and it's from the people who are not getting paid to do that work. They just want us to have the best of experience in our learning. 

I can't believe such a culture can exist let alone grow. It's... simply amazing. Goodness is a force to be reckoned with.

#  Final thoughts 

The course is designed very well (I can tell it has went through a LOT of iterations). I would recommend everyone gives this a try (I know it can feel like an ads but it's really was one of the best (and hardest) experience of my life).

You can find more information about the course at [their website](https://www.dhamma.org/). Depends on your country of resident, there should be a local website for your country where you can find courses at meditation centers.

If you decide to go, I hope you have the best of experience.
